# Tom Renard, Senior Product Engineer

<Lead>
  I build the customer facing parts of products, and I own what happens after
  they ship.
</Lead>

Six years in, mostly React and TypeScript, mostly Berlin. I studied management before I wrote code, which is probably why I care more about whether a feature moved anything than about how it was built.

## Now

I work at [Aroundhome](https://www.aroundhome.de) in Berlin, in a small team that owns the flow from someone describing a home project to getting connected with companies who can do it.

I take features from the first conversation to the number they move. That means shaping the requirement with product and design, building it wherever it needs to be built, and then owning the rollout and the read on whether it worked. Most recently that was moving the contact step onto the brand site the visitor started on, instead of sending them to our main domain: frontend, backend and infrastructure in one feature.

I would rather give an honest read that says not yet than a win that does not hold up.

## What I am good at

Frontend is where I am deepest. React and TypeScript, and the parts people skip: loading and error states, edge cases, accessibility, the behaviour of a form when the data is wrong. I go into the Go and Ruby services when the problem lives there, and into the infrastructure when it lives there instead.

The other half is knowing which feature is worth building, and being able to argue for it with a PM, a designer or a stakeholder who does not care about the code.

## How I work with AI

I use coding agents all day. Most of my value now is in review and verification rather than typing, so I spend the time on what they get wrong: state boundaries, edge cases, and whether the thing actually does what the ticket asked for.

My side projects make that explicit. Deterministic scripts do the arithmetic, the model only does judgement, and in one of them the build refuses to publish if a claim is not sourced. Guardrails first, then let the agent run.
